### 1.2 Standalone Manifesto-Style Essays

**"Moore's Law for Everything"** (March 16, 2021)
- URL: https://moores.samaltman.com/
- **Thesis**: AI will generate immense wealth; society must pre-adapt via tax policy. Proposes "American Equity Fund" — taxing companies at 2.5% market value and land at 2.5% value, distributing proceeds as direct equity ownership to all citizens.
- **Key concept**: "Moore's Law for everything" — housing, education, food, clothing becoming half as expensive every two years.
- **Key line**: "The best way to improve capitalism is to enable everyone to benefit from it directly as an equity owner."
- **Credibility**: PRIMARY

**"The Intelligence Age"** (September 23, 2024)
- URL: https://ia.samaltman.com/
- **Thesis**: "Deep learning worked, got predictably better with scale, and we dedicated increasing resources to it." Superintelligence possible in "a few thousand days." Predicts fixing climate, space colonies, discovery of all physics.
- **Key concept**: "Intelligence Age" as fourth great epoch (Stone → Agricultural → Industrial → Intelligence).
- **Key line**: "Technology brought us from the Stone Age to the Agricultural Age and then to the Industrial Age. From here, the path to the Intelligence Age is paved with compute, energy, and human will."
- **Credibility**: PRIMARY

## 3. RECURRING THEMES (appearing 3+ times across writings)

### 3.1 Core Doctrines

1. **Scaling Laws as Destiny**
   - "The intelligence of an AI model roughly equals the log of the resources used to train and run it" (Three Observations)
   - "Deep learning worked, got predictably better with scale" (The Intelligence Age)
   - "You can spend arbitrary amounts of money and get continuous and predictable gains" (Three Observations)
   - Appears in: Three Observations, The Intelligence Age, The Gentle Singularity, Reflections, Planning for AGI and beyond

2. **Gradual Transition / Iterative Deployment**
   - "A gradual transition to a world with AGI is better than a sudden one" (Planning for AGI)
   - "Iteratively and gradually releasing it into the world, giving society time to adapt and co-evolve" (Reflections)
   - "One of our reasons for launching products early and often is to give society and the technology time to co-evolve" (Three Observations)
   - Appears in: Planning for AGI, Reflections, Three Observations, Our Principles, The Gentle Singularity

3. **Cost Plummets → Universal Access**
   - "The cost to use a given level of AI falls about 10x every 12 months" (Three Observations)
   - "Moore's Law for everything" — everything becomes half as expensive every two years
   - "Intelligence too cheap to meter is well within grasp" (The Gentle Singularity)
   - Appears in: Three Observations, Moore's Law for Everything, The Intelligence Age, The Gentle Singularity

4. **Abundant Intelligence + Abundant Energy = Anything**
   - "Intelligence and energy... are going to become wildly abundant. These two have been the fundamental limiters on human progress" (The Gentle Singularity)
   - "With nearly-limitless intelligence and abundant energy... we can do quite a lot" (The Intelligence Age)
   - Appears in: The Intelligence Age, The Gentle Singularity, Three Observations

5. **Capital Over Labor / Equity Distribution**
   - "Taxing capital rather than labor... enable everyone to benefit from it directly as an equity owner" (Moore's Law for Everything)
   - "The balance of power between capital and labor could easily get messed up" (Three Observations)
   - "American Equity Fund" proposal — 2.5% tax on companies and land
   - Appears in: Moore's Law for Everything, American Equity, Three Observations, The Software Revolution

6. **"Hard Startups" Advantage**
   - "It's often easier to succeed with a hard startup than an easy one" (Hard Startups)
   - "A hard startup is a tailwind" (Hard Startups)
   - "It is easier for a team to do a hard thing that really matters than to do an easy thing" (What I Wish Someone Had Told Me)
   - Appears in: Hard Startups, What I Wish Someone Had Told Me, How To Be Successful

7. **Compound Growth / Long-Term Thinking**
   - "Compounding is magic. Look for it everywhere." (How To Be Successful)
   - "The furthest out years are the most important" (How To Be Successful)
   - "Plans should be measured in decades, execution should be measured in weeks" (What I Wish Someone Had Told Me)
   - Appears in: How To Be Successful, What I Wish Someone Had Told Me, The Gentle Singularity

8. **Self-Belief as Prerequisite**
   - "The most successful people I know believe in themselves almost to the point of delusion" (How To Be Successful)
   - Appears in: How To Be Successful, What I Wish Someone Had Told Me, Reflections

### 3.2 Recurring Framing Devices

- **"X worked" — short declarative summaries** (e.g., "Deep learning worked" = entire thesis of The Intelligence Age in 3 words)
- **Technological epoch sequence**: Stone Age → Agricultural → Industrial → (Software/Intelligence)
- **"Wonders become routine, then table stakes"** (The Gentle Singularity)
- **Co-evolution framing**: Society and AI must evolve together; deployment is the teacher
- **Positive-sum games**: "As a society, we will be back in an expanding world, and we can again focus on playing positive-sum games"

## 6. CONTRADICTIONS AND TENSIONS (Recorded as-is, not smoothed)

### 6.1 Speed vs. Safety

- **Written (2023, Planning for AGI):** "a slower takeoff is easier to make safe" and "coordination among AGI efforts to slow down at critical junctures will likely be important."
- **Written (2025, Three Observations):** "we see no reason for exponentially increasing investment to stop." No mention of slowing down.
- **Action (2024-2026):** Reportedly raised ~$10B+ from Microsoft, pursued $7T chip manufacturing plans, rapidly deployed GPT-4, GPT-4o, o1, o3.
- **Observation (secondary):** Critics note his compute-expansion plans directly contradict his stated preference for slower takeoff. The LessWrong/EA analysis: "Altman's plan seems to fly in the face of the arguments he made not even one year ago."
- **Altman's counter-argument:** Safety and capabilities must progress together; iterating in the real world produces safety knowledge.

### 6.2 Democratization vs. Power Consolidation

- **Written (2026, Our Principles):** "We will resist the potential of this technology to consolidate power in the hands of the few."
- **Written (2026, personal blog):** "The only solution... orient towards sharing the technology with people broadly, and for no one to have the ring."
- **Action:** After November 2023 board crisis, Altman returned with a board reshaped to align with him; the New Yorker (April 2026) described "a relentless will to power."
- **Action:** OpenAI served legal notices to at least 7 advocacy groups questioning its restructure.
- **Action:** Pentagon deal (Feb 2026) — critics called it "safety theater" (Dario Amodei's reported comment), with non-binding promises against autonomous weapons and mass surveillance.

### 6.3 Open Source vs. Controlled Access

- **Early OpenAI (2015-2017):** Committed to open-sourcing everything; Altman originally believed in full transparency.
- **Written (2023, Planning for AGI):** "we now believe we were wrong in our original thinking about openness... pivoted from thinking we should release everything to thinking that we should figure out how to safely share access."
- **Tension:** The pivot is framed as safety-driven, but critics note it also enables commercial moats.

### 6.4 Nonprofit Mission vs. For-Profit Structure

- **Written (OpenAI Charter):** Capped returns for shareholders, nonprofit governance with ability to override for-profit interests.
- **Action (2024-2026):** OpenAI transitioned to for-profit structure, removed cap on returns, Altman received equity. Company now pursuing IPO.
- **Written (2026, personal blog):** "we need to operate in a more predictable way now" — acknowledging the platform shift.

### 6.5 AGI Definition Ambiguity

- **Written (Three Observations):** "AGI is a weakly defined term, but generally speaking we mean it to be a system that can tackle increasingly complex problems, at human level, in many fields."
- **Written (OpenAI Charter):** "the board determines when we've attained AGI."
- **Tension:** Altman simultaneously claims AGI is coming soon (a few thousand days) while keeping definition flexible enough that the board (now aligned with him) determines when it's achieved — creating a self-serving definition.

### 6.6 "Safety Progress vs Capability Progress Ratio"

- **Written (Planning for AGI):** "It's important that the ratio of safety progress to capability progress increases."
- **Written (same post):** "We think we often have to make progress on AI safety and capabilities together. It's a false dichotomy."
- **Action:** Superalignment team reportedly received fraction of promised 20% compute allocation, and outdated hardware (Fortune report). Team leader Ilya Sutskever left.

## 9. WRITING STYLE CHARACTERISTICS

1. **Short sentences, declarative.** Entire thesis often in 3 words ("Deep learning worked") or 15 words.
2. **List-based.** "13 thoughts," "17 things," "3 observations" — numbered lists are his signature format.
3. **Self-deprecating humility.** "We are much better at research than we are at naming things" (ChatGPT naming). "I am a flawed person."
4. **Certainty about direction, uncertainty about timing.** "I'm confident we'll get there" but "it may take longer."
5. **Historical framing.** Always places current moment in grand historical arc (Stone Age → Agricultural → Industrial → Intelligence).
6. **Acknowledgments lists.** Unusually long acknowledgment sections citing advisors and draft readers.
7. **All-lowercase on X/Twitter.** Distinctive casual style ("i never took the dead internet theory that seriously").
8. **Absence of technical depth.** Unlike Ilya Sutskever or Dario Amodei, Altman rarely writes technical details; writes at the strategic/societal/policy level.
9. **Admission of error.** Repeated pattern: "we were wrong about X" (openness, structure, expectations). Serves both genuine learning and rhetorical inoculation.
10. **Optimism as framing device.** Even when discussing existential risk, ultimate conclusion is positive. "The future can be almost unimaginably great."
