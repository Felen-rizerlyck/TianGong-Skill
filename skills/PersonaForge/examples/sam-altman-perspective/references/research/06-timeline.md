# Sam Altman: Complete Timeline (1985–2026)

## Birth & Early Life

### 1985
- **Apr 22**: Samuel Harris Altman born in Chicago, Illinois, to a Jewish American family. Mother Connie Gibstine is a dermatologist; father Jerry Altman is a real estate broker. Eldest of four siblings (brothers Max and Jack, sister Ann). Paternal great-grandfather from Płock, Poland.
  - Source: Wikipedia / Britannica | *High credibility*

### 1989
- Family moves to Clayton, Missouri (father's hometown), a suburb of St. Louis.
  - Source: Britannica / New Yorker profile | *High credibility*

### 1993 (age 8)
- Receives first computer (Apple Macintosh); begins coding, disassembling and examining computer hardware.
  - Source: New Yorker (2016) / Wikipedia | *High credibility*

### ~1999–2003
- Attends John Burroughs School, an elite private prep school in Ladue, Missouri.
- Comes out as gay in a school assembly; encourages teachers to post "Safe Space" placards. College counselor later tells New Yorker: "What Sam did changed the school."
  - Source: New Yorker (2016) | *High credibility*

## Stanford & Loopt

### 2003–2005
- Attends Stanford University, studies computer science.
- Drops out after two years (2005). Later says he learned more playing poker with classmates than from lectures. Poker taught him "how to notice patterns in people over time, how to make decisions with very imperfect information."
  - Source: New York Times (2023), New Yorker (2016), Wikipedia | *High credibility*

### 2005 (age 19)
- Co-founds **Loopt**, a location-based social networking mobile app. Loopt is among the first companies funded by Y Combinator.
  - Source: Wikipedia, Britannica | *High credibility*

### 2012
- Loopt acquired by Green Dot Corporation for $43.4 million. The app failed to attract significant users despite partnerships with Sprint.
  - Source: Wikipedia, Britannica | *High credibility*

## Y Combinator Era

### 2011
- Begins working part-time as a partner at Y Combinator.
  - Source: Wikipedia, Britannica | *High credibility*

### 2012
- Founds venture fund **Hydrazine Capital** with brother Max Altman.
  - Source: Wikipedia, Britannica | *High credibility*

### Feb 2014
- Appointed **President of Y Combinator**, succeeding co-founder Paul Graham.
- Also serves as CEO of Reddit for 8 days in 2014 (after YC acquires Reddit, briefly steps in).
  - Source: Wikipedia, Y Combinator, Reddit archives | *High credibility*

### 2014–2019 (YC Presidency)
- Transforms YC from a small startup accelerator into a global powerhouse.
- Expands batch sizes, creates YC Continuity (growth-stage fund), YC Research, and YC China (briefly).
- Under his tenure, YC funds ~1,900 companies including Airbnb, Stripe, Instacart, DoorDash, Reddit, Twitch, Cruise, Coinbase.
- Launches the "Startup School" and online versions of the program.
- Reviews >100 AI startups during this period, building pattern recognition that informs his OpenAI leadership.
  - Source: Wikipedia, Y Combinator blog, Forbes, Britannica | *High credibility*

### Mar 2019
- Transitions from YC President to **Chairman of the Board** at Y Combinator. Hands operational duties to Geoffrey Ralston and later Michael Seibel.
- Simultaneously becomes full-time **CEO of OpenAI**.
  - Source: Y Combinator, Wikipedia | *High credibility*

## OpenAI: Founding & Early Years

### Dec 2015
- **OpenAI founded** as a nonprofit AI research organization in San Francisco.
- Co-founders: Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Ilya Sutskever, Greg Brockman, Wojciech Zaremba, John Schulman, Trevor Blackwell, Vicki Cheung, Jessica Livingston, Durk Kingma, Pamela Vagata, Andrej Karpathy.
- Altman and Musk serve as co-chairs. Initial funding: $1 billion pledged by Altman, Musk, Peter Thiel, Amazon Web Services, Infosys, YC Research.
- Mission: "To advance digital intelligence in the way that is most likely to benefit humanity as a whole, unconstrained by a need to generate financial return."
  - Source: OpenAI blog, Wikipedia | *High credibility*

### 2016
- OpenAI releases **OpenAI Gym**, a toolkit for reinforcement learning research.
- Publishes research on reinforcement learning and generative models.
  - Source: OpenAI research blog | *High credibility*

### 2018
- **Elon Musk leaves OpenAI board**. Musk proposed taking control of OpenAI to compete with Google; Altman declined. Musk leaves, cutting off critical funding.
- OpenAI releases first **Generative Pre-trained Transformer (GPT-1)** paper: "Improving Language Understanding by Generative Pre-Training."
- **OpenAI Five** project demonstrates 5 AI agents playing Dota 2 at professional level.
- Recognition grows that advancing toward AGI requires massive compute, far beyond nonprofit funding capacity.
  - Source: Britannica, OpenAI blog, Chamath Palihapitiya (2023) | *High credibility*

### 2019 (Key Turning Point)
- OpenAI transitions from pure nonprofit to **"capped-profit" model** (OpenAI LP). Creates a for-profit subsidiary controlled by the nonprofit board. Investor returns capped at 100x.
- **Microsoft invests $1 billion**, provides Azure cloud computing, integrates OpenAI tech into products.
- **Altman becomes full-time CEO** of OpenAI (steps down as YC president same month).
- Altman tells NYT OpenAI's ambition is on the scale of the **Manhattan Project**. Notes he shares a birthday with J. Robert Oppenheimer.
- **GPT-2** released (initially withheld due to safety concerns, then released in full).
  - Source: Britannica, OpenAI blog, New York Times, Wikipedia | *High credibility*

### 2020
- **GPT-3** released (June 2020). 175 billion parameters. Demonstrates remarkable language capabilities.
- OpenAI launches commercial API for GPT-3.
  - Source: OpenAI blog | *High credibility*

### 2021
- **DALL-E** introduced (Jan 2021): text-to-image generation.
- **GPT-3** fine-tuning and broader API access.
  - Source: OpenAI blog | *High credibility*

### 2022
- **Nov 30: ChatGPT launched**. Reaches 1 million users in 5 days. Becomes the fastest-growing consumer application in history (100M users in 2 months).
- Triggers global AI boom and public awareness of generative AI.
  - Source: OpenAI, industry reporting | *High credibility*

### 2023 (Pre-Crisis)
- **GPT-4** released (Mar 2023). Multimodal capabilities, improved reasoning.
- **DALL-E 2** and later **DALL-E 3** integrated with ChatGPT.
- Altman testifies before US Senate (May 2023), calling for AI regulation.
- **Worldcoin** launches token and mainnet (July 24, 2023). Altman co-founded Tools for Humanity in 2019 alongside Alex Blania and Max Novendstern.
- Altman participates in UK AI Safety Summit (Nov 2023) alongside Musk, Harris, Sunak.
  - Source: Wikipedia, Britannica, Congressional records | *High credibility*

## The November 2023 OpenAI Board Crisis

### Nov 16 (Thursday)
- Altman receives text from Ilya Sutskever asking to join a Google Meet the next day.
  - Source: Greg Brockman's X posts, multiple journalism sources | *High credibility*

### Nov 17 (Friday) — "Fired Friday"
- OpenAI board fires Altman via Google Meet. Statement: "Mr. Altman's departure follows a deliberative review process by the board, which concluded that he was not consistently candid in his communications with the board, hindering its ability to exercise its responsibilities."
- CTO **Mira Murati** appointed interim CEO.
- Board at time: Ilya Sutskever, Adam D'Angelo (Quora CEO), Tasha McCauley (GeoSim Systems), Helen Toner (Georgetown).
- **Greg Brockman** resigns as president in solidarity.
- Three senior researchers (Jakub Pachocki, Aleksander Madry, Szymon Sidor) also resign.
- Altman posts on X: "i loved my time at openai. it was transformative for me personally, and hopefully the world a little bit. most of all i loved working with such talented people."
  - Source: OpenAI blog, Mashable timeline, NPR, Bloomberg | *High credibility*

### Nov 18 (Saturday) — Negotiations Begin
- Major investors (Microsoft, Thrive Capital, Tiger Global) pressure board to reverse decision.
- Board reportedly reaches out to Altman to negotiate return.
- Saturday night deadline passes without a deal.
- OpenAI employees begin publicly supporting Altman.
  - Source: Forbes, The Verge | *High credibility*

### Nov 19 (Sunday) — Stalemate
- Altman posts photo wearing OpenAI guest badge: "First and last time i ever wear one of these."
- Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella personally involved in negotiations.
- OpenAI announces **Emmett Shear** (former Twitch CEO) as new interim CEO — replacing Murati.
- Deal for Altman's return appears dead.
  - Source: Mashable, The Information, The Verge | *High credibility*

### Nov 20 (Monday) — Microsoft Pivot
- **Nadella announces** Altman and Brockman will join Microsoft to lead a new advanced AI research division.
- Over 500 of ~770 OpenAI employees sign an open letter threatening to resign unless board resigns and Altman returns.
- **Ilya Sutskever** dramatically reverses: "I deeply regret my participation in the board's actions. I never intended to harm OpenAI."
- Nadella gives ambiguous interview to CNBC when asked if Altman is actually going to be a Microsoft employee.
  - Source: Microsoft press release, CNBC, Reuters, The Verge | *High credibility*

### Nov 21 (Tuesday) — Altman Returns
- OpenAI announces agreement: **Altman returns as CEO**.
- New initial board: **Bret Taylor** (Chair, former Salesforce CEO), **Larry Summers** (former Treasury Secretary), **Adam D'Angelo** (retained from old board).
- Board members Sutskever, McCauley, Toner step down. Independent investigation into Altman's conduct announced.
- Essentially full victory for Altman: board that fired him is gone, employees unified behind him.
  - Source: OpenAI announcement, New York Times, NPR | *High credibility*

### Aftermath (Late 2023–2024)
- New board expanded: includes Microsoft (observer seat, later relinquished), ex-Salesforce CEO Bret Taylor (Chair), Larry Summers, Adam D'Angelo, Fidji Simo (Instacart CEO), Nicole Seligman (ex-Sony GC), Paul Nakasone (retired US Army general, former NSA director).
- Sutskever's role diminished; subsequently left OpenAI (announced departure May 2024).
- Microsoft gets non-voting observer seat (Feb 2024); Microsoft later gives up this seat (July 2024) amid antitrust scrutiny.
- Independent investigation of Altman's conduct reportedly clears him but prompts governance reforms.
  - Source: Reuters, The Verge, TechCrunch | *High credibility*

## Worldcoin / Tools for Humanity

### 2019
- Altman co-founds **Tools for Humanity** with Alex Blania and Max Novendstern. Develops Worldcoin, a cryptocurrency project using iris biometrics for identity verification.
  - Source: Worldcoin whitepaper, TechCrunch | *Medium credibility*

### 2021–2022
- Worldcoin begins pilot deployments. Collects iris scans using custom "Orb" devices.
- Raises ~$125M from investors including a16z, Digital Currency Group, Coinbase Ventures, Reid Hoffman.
  - Source: Bloomberg, Forbes, The Verge | *Medium credibility*

### Jul 2023
- **Worldcoin mainnet and WLD token launch**. Users receive tokens for iris scan verification.
- Launches simultaneously in 20+ countries.
- Rapid regulatory pushback: Kenya bans Worldcoin, suspends operations (Aug 2023); UK, France, Germany, Argentina, Spain open investigations.
- Privacy concerns: critics argue iris data collection raises surveillance risks; Worldcoin claims data is encrypted and privacy-preserving using zero-knowledge proofs.
  - Source: Worldcoin blog, Reuters, BBC, Bloomberg | *High credibility*

### 2024–2025
- Worldcoin rebrands to **"World"** (Dec 2024). Shifts focus toward proof-of-personhood and identity verification rather than cryptocurrency.
- Regulatory challenges continue: Spanish ban upheld temporarily; Hong Kong, South Korea investigations.
- By 2025: over 15 million people verified across 160+ countries.
- Altman remains less directly involved day-to-day; Alex Blania serves as CEO.
  - Source: World blog, TechCrunch, The Verge | *Medium credibility*

## Intellectual Evolution: Key View Changes

### 2015 — AI Optimism & Openness
- OpenAI launched as fully open, non-profit. Altman believed open research would best benefit humanity.
- "OpenAI" named to reflect commitment to openness: would publish all research openly.

### 2019 — Pragmatic Shift
- Transition to capped-profit model. Altman's view evolves: AGI development requires more capital than nonprofit structure allows.
- Openness gives way to competitive secrecy as commercial incentives grow.

### 2022–2023 — Accelerationist Turn
- With ChatGPT's explosive success, Altman becomes more aggressively pro-development.
- Shifts from "move carefully with safety" to "move fast but responsibly."
- Testifies before Senate (May 2023): calls for regulation while simultaneously racing to deploy.

### 2023 (Post-Crisis) — Governance & Control
- After board crisis, consolidates power. Emerges as the most dominant figure in AI.
- Adopts rhetoric of "building safely" while accelerating deployment. Critics call his stance contradictory.
- Distances from effective altruist (EA) safety movement that influenced early OpenAI board members.

### 2024–2026 — Infrastructure & Superintelligence
- Increasingly focuses on compute and energy as the binding constraints on AI development.
- Advocates for massive infrastructure build: $850B-$1T in data centers and energy.
- Begins publicly discussing AGI as near-term (within decade). Distinguishes AGI from superintelligence.
- More explicitly addresses job displacement and proposes "Universal Basic Compute" as solution.
- Relationship with Microsoft evolves from exclusive partnership to multi-cloud, more independent posture.
  - Source: CNBC, Politico (Sep 2025), NeuraPlus (2026), Britannica | *Medium–High credibility*
