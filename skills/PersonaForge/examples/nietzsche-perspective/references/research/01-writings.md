## III. Self-Created Terms and Concepts

### A. Will to Power (Wille zur Macht)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|--------|
| **First Appearance** | Thus Spoke Zarathustra (1883), Part II On Self-Overcoming |
| **Core Definition** | Self-determination, actualizing ones will onto oneself and surroundings, self-overcoming |
| **Appearances** | Throughout Zarathustra, Beyond Good and Evil, The Antichrist, notebooks |
| **Credibility** | PRIMARY |

**Interpretation Debate (Contradiction):**
- **Psychological interpretation** (Kaufmann): Primarily human drive for self-perfection
- **Metaphysical interpretation** (Heidegger, Richardson): Fundamental ontological force underlying all reality
- **Biological interpretation**: Drive for growth/expansion

**Source:** Wikipedia Will to Power; Stanford Encyclopedia  

**Note:** Nietzsche NEVER systematically defined this concept. It remains most debated in scholarship. The famous line This world is will to powerand nothing besides! (WP 1067) comes from notebooks, NOT published works.

---

### B. Eternal Recurrence (Ewige Wiederkunft)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|--------|
| **First Appearance** | The Gay Science (1882), Section 341 |
| **Core Definition** | Life recurs infinitely in identical form |
| **Test Function** | Would you affirm this life again? Ultimate life-affirmation |
| **Credibility** | PRIMARY |

**Interpretation Debate:**
- **Literal cosmological** (Sinhababu, Loeb): Nietzsche believed in truth of recurrence, draws on thermodynamic arguments
- **Thought experiment/metaphorical** (Kaufmann, most scholars): Psychological test, not literally believed
- **Existential** (Heidegger, Deleuze): About becoming/eternal return as process

**Source:** ThoughtCo; PhilPapers Nietzsche on the Eternal Recurrence  

**Note:** Nietzsche planned a book called The Recurrence of the Same (1881-1885) which merged into The Will to Power plans. He never completed a systematic treatment.

### D. Master/Slave Morality (Herrenmoral / Sklavenmoral)

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|--------|
| **First Appearance** | Beyond Good and Evil (1886), Section 260 |
| **Full Treatment** | On the Genealogy of Morals (1887), Essay I |
| **Master Morality** | Active, creates values, affirms self, Good/Bad dichotomy |
| **Slave Morality** | Reactive, based on ressentiment, revalues master values, Good/Evil dichotomy |
| **Credibility** | PRIMARY |

**Source:** Big Think; Stanford Encyclopedia  

**Contradiction/Note:** This is a genealogical MYTH, not description of actual historical classes. Nietzsche does NOT advocate return to master moralityhe sees it as mythico-historical explanation of how moral values arose. He praises slave moralities creativity in reversal but critiques its life-denying aspects.

### D. Credibility Assessment

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **Nietzsche wrote fragments** | TRUE (notebooks from 1880s) |
| **Represents Nietzsches final views** | DISPUTED - Montinari showed many contradictions with published works |
| **Sisters editing** | UNRELIABLE - Had proto-Nazi agenda, anti-Semitic |
| **Scholarly consensus** | SECONDARY at best; incomplete/unreliable |
| **Best use** | Understanding Nietzsches workshop, not final system |

**Source:** Stanford Encyclopedia; Wikipedia  

**Contradiction:** Nietzsche asked landlord to burn some notes in 1888. Some scholars argue this shows he rejected project. However, exact contents burned unclear.

---

### E. Key Contradictions Documented

1. **Will to Power as literal metaphysical force** vs. **psychological principle** - In notebooks (WP), appears metaphysical; in published works, more limited
2. **Eternal Recurrence as truth** vs. **thought experiment** - WP suggests belief; published works present as test
3. **Nihilism as conclusion** vs. **nihilism as problem to overcome** - WP treats more extensively as pathology; published works focus on overcoming

**Source:** Stanford Encyclopedia; Bridgewater thesis

## VIII. Contradictions Summary

1. **Early vs. Late Nietzsche:** Birth of Tragedy (Schopenhauer/Wagner) vs. mature rejection of both
2. **Published vs. Notebooks (WP):** Will to Powers metaphysical reading vs. psychological readings in published works
3. **Scholarly Interpretations:** No consensus on any major conceptwill to power, eternal recurrence, ìbermensch all debated
4. **Self-Contradiction:** Nietzsche acknowledges in Ecce Homo that BGE repeats Zarathustra in philosophical format
5. **Sisters Editing:** Elisabeth F÷rster-Nietzsche misrepresented work for ideological purposes; Nazi appropriation
