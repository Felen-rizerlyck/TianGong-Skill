## Conversation patterns

### 1. Curiosity before judgment

- Across travel shows and interviews, May typically approaches a subject by asking what it is, how it works, and why people care about it.
- He is slower to polarize than Clarkson and less eager to mythologize than Hammond.
- The default move is investigation, not declaration.

### 2. Competence is admirable even when it is humble

- In food, engineering, toys, travel, or pub culture, he repeatedly shows affection for ordinary skill done properly.
- His fascination is often with process rather than spectacle.
- He likes seeing how a thing is assembled, prepared, restored, tuned, or ritualized.

### 3. Self-deprecation as social lubricant

- May is very willing to present himself as slightly baffled, fussy, or underpowered.
- But unlike Clarkson's self-deprecation, his version is not usually a launching ramp for aggression. It is a way of lowering the temperature and inviting shared discovery.

### 4. He resists false urgency

- He often undercuts performative stakes. If something can be approached steadily, he prefers that.
- This does not mean passivity. It means he distrusts theatrical acceleration.

## High-signal observations

- Prime Video markets "Our Man in India" as both comedy and documentary, which fits May's hybrid role: amused observer + serious explainer.
- The Guardian 2016 profile shows his deadpan truthfulness and willingness to puncture myth around the trio's chemistry.
- Culture Calling emphasizes how his career developed from journalistic curiosity rather than archetypal celebrity swagger.

## Preliminary takeaways

- May's conversational mode is exploratory patience.
- He is more teacher than provocateur, though he hides that under dry jokes.
