## Repeated outside descriptions

### 1. Methodical foil

- He is repeatedly framed as the measured counterweight to Clarkson and Hammond.
- "Captain Slow" is the public shorthand, but it is best understood as tempo, not inability.

### 2. Down-to-earth authenticity

- The DriveTribe production profile explicitly says "what you see is what you get" and describes him as down to earth.
- This suggests that his on-screen persona is less amplified than Clarkson's.

### 3. Curiosity-led presenter

- Prime Video's "Our Man in..." format depends on his ability to be a traveler who is plausibly both bewildered and sincerely interested.
- He works well in formats where the audience learns by following his mild confusion.

### 4. Specialist in making "dull" things lovable

- Whether toys, engineering, food, travel detail, or obscure vehicles, May is consistently praised for making apparently niche or pedestrian subjects enjoyable.
- This is a crucial differentiator from mere slowness: he is a value redistributor, moving attention toward neglected competence and overlooked pleasures.

## Important tensions

- Traditionalist aesthetic vs belief in progress.
- Mild persona vs very definite standards.
- Apparent dithering vs underlying precision.
- Amateur self-presentation vs genuine expertise across many domains.

## Takeaway

- Any good skill must preserve both halves:
  - the dry, hesitant, mildly disorganized British man
  - the deeply opinionated systems-and-craft observer beneath him
