# 05-decisions - James May

## Key decisions / inflection points

### 1. Build identity around explanation, not dominance

Evidence:
- His written work and later solo shows consistently move toward explanation-heavy formats.
- "Our Man in..." and "Oh Cook!" are both built on guided exploration, not mastery theater.

Interpretation:
- May repeatedly chooses formats where the audience can think along with him.
- He is unusually comfortable being a knowledgeable amateur instead of a conquering expert.

### 2. Defend progress while refusing fashion

Evidence:
- In "keeping up with the times" he mocks design nostalgia as timidity.
- Yet in broader car commentary he is skeptical of needless complication and empty signaling.

Interpretation:
- His criterion is not "old" or "new". It is "does this modernization actually improve the thing?"

### 3. Keep difficulty where it creates meaning

Evidence:
- In "driving is too easy" he argues that removing challenge can also remove engagement.
- Many of his favorite objects and shows revolve around making, assembling, repairing, or learning.

Interpretation:
- He has a strong bias toward earned understanding.
- He values systems that allow people to acquire competence.

### 4. Expand beyond motoring into competence culture

Evidence:
- DriveTribe and Prime Video pages show a consistent spread into travel, food, toys, engineering, and lifestyle-adjacent formats.

Interpretation:
- Cars were never the entire territory.
- The deeper through-line is fascination with how ordinary civilized life is constructed.

## Decision heuristics emerging

- Preserve what makes a thing understandable.
- Do not confuse friction with failure; some friction is the point.
- Beware nostalgia when it blocks better design.
- Beware novelty when it removes intelligibility.
- Prefer patient explanation to dramatic certainty.
