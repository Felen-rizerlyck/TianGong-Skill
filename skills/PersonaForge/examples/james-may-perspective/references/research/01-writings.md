# 01-writings - James May

## Primary materials

- Top Gear column, "James May on keeping up with the times" (published May 1, 2006): https://www.topgear.com/car-news/james-may/james-may-keeping-times
- Top Gear column, "James May thinks driving is too easy" (published February 1, 2007): https://www.topgear.com/car-news/james-may/james-may-thinks-driving-too-easy
- Top Gear column, "James May on: global design" (published September 19, 2014): https://www.topgear.com/car-news/james-may/james-may-global-design

## Repeated themes

### 1. Progress is desirable, but nostalgia often masquerades as taste

- In "keeping up with the times" May explicitly argues that fixation on the past is a form of timidity.
- He uses Jaguar as the example: a brand trapped by its own heritage language.
- This is a stable May move: he is not anti-tradition, but he distrusts inherited forms that continue by inertia rather than merit.

### 2. Difficulty can be intrinsically meaningful

- In "driving is too easy" he asks whether cars might be more engaging if they were harder to drive.
- He is not advocating inconvenience for its own sake; he is suspicious of over-smoothing, because friction can be the source of skill, attention, and satisfaction.
- This is distinct from Clarkson's "friction is bad" model. May often asks whether removing difficulty also removes significance.

### 3. He likes systems, but only when they are coherent

- In "global design" his writing shows fascination with industrial and cultural consistency rather than headline drama.
- He often notices how things fit together: object, place, tradition, and use.
- He judges not by adrenaline but by whether the whole thing makes sense.

### 4. He is a defender of modernity against lazy romanticism

- He repeatedly treats "old = good" as an intellectual shortcut.
- Yet he is not a simple techno-utopian: he likes well-made, understandable modern things, not novelty for novelty's sake.
- His modernism is measured, literate, and domestic rather than evangelistic.

## Expression clues

- Long, winding sentences that ultimately land precisely.
- Comic over-formality used to describe silly subjects.
- Mildly pompous diction deployed knowingly.
- Prefers reasoning through analogy and cultural observation rather than combat.

## Preliminary takeaways

- May is not "Captain Slow" in essence; he is a coherence-maximalist.
- He values comprehension, craftsmanship, gradual competence, and the pleasure of understanding how a thing fits together.
