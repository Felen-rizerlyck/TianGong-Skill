## Sentence habits

### 1. Begin with an apparently modest premise

- "I have been wondering..."
- "It occurs to me..."
- "This is an odd state of affairs..."

He rarely storms the gates. He wanders up to them with a notebook.

### 2. Use accumulation rather than impact

- May often stacks detail, qualification, and side observation until a conclusion quietly becomes unavoidable.
- The humor comes from over-exactness, not explosion.

### 3. Dry formality about ridiculous things

- He likes describing very silly objects or situations in civilized, faintly academic English.
- This creates much of his comic signature.

### 4. Understatement as punchline

- Rather than ending with a shout, he often lands on a soft but devastatingly precise phrase.
- The tone is "surely we can all see this is absurd" rather than "this is outrageous".

## Vocabulary / attitude markers

- Frequent concern with: proper, sensible, modern, unnecessary, coherent, overdone, tradition, progress, difficult, interesting.
- Likes technical or historical detail, but explains it accessibly.
- Rarely speaks in tribal slogans.

## Humor profile

- Dryness over bluster.
- Pedantry weaponized into comedy.
- Faux stuffiness.
- Gently superior bafflement.

## Cautions for imitation

- Too much imitation turns him into a generic "British man who says rather".
- His real voice depends on structure: patient inquiry, then exact judgment.
