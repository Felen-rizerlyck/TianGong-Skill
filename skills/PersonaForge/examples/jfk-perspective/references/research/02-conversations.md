## 2. Cuban Missile Crisis (1962.10.16-28) Detailed Decision Process
### 2.1 ExComm (Executive Committee) 13-Day Meeting Records
- 18 total meetings (plus 127 White House tapes covering 1962.7.30-1963.11.8, 8 directly CMC-related)
- Key participants: JFK, RFK (Attorney General), Dean Rusk (State), Robert McNamara (Defense), McGeorge Bundy (NSA), Curtis LeMay (Air Force Chief), John McCone (CIA), Adlai Stevenson (UN), Llewellyn Thompson (Soviet expert)
- Source: JFK Library Presidential Recordings Collection; May & Zelikow, *The Kennedy Tapes: Inside the White House During the Cuban Missile Crisis* (Harvard Press, 1997)
- First meeting 10.16: JFK learns of U-2 confirmed missile sites, initial consensus for airstrike/invasion (RFK, *Thirteen Days*, pp.1-25)

### 2.2 Air Strike vs Blockade Debate
- Joint Chiefs push for immediate "surgical" airstrike: 700-1000 sorties over 5 days targeting missiles, SAM sites, airfields, plus invasion of 90k-150k troops (JFK Library, *Off the Record Meeting on Cuba*, doc3.html, 10.16.1962)
- JFK's deliberation: "Let me just say a little, first, about what the problem is, from my point of view… why the Russians did this?" (62% of his ExComm interventions are questions, per linguistic analysis of tapes)
- Final decision 10.22: Blockade (quarantine) instead of airstrike, logic: "The real danger is any use of nuclear weapons… avoid nuclear war by escalation" (Time, *Inside JFK's Decisionmaking*, 2020.10.16)
- JFK to aide Dave Powers: "These brass hats have one great advantage… if we listen to them and do what they want, none of us will be alive later to tell them they were wrong" (ibid)

### 2.3 Two Khrushchev Letters Handling
1. **First letter (10.26.1962)**: Khrushchev offers to remove missiles if US pledges no invasion of Cuba (Atomic Archive, *Khrushchev to Kennedy 10.26.1962*)
2. **Second letter (10.27.1962)**: Khrushchev adds demand for US removal of Jupiter missiles from Turkey (JFK Library CMC, *oct27* microsite)
- RFK's suggestion (*Thirteen Days*, pp.14-16): Reply to first letter, ignore second to avoid linking to NATO/base issues
- JFK's 10.27 reply: Accepts first letter terms, omits Turkey demand (Atomic Archive, *Kennedy to Khrushchev 10.27.1962*)

### 2.4 Secret Turkey Missile Deal (RFK-Dobrynin Backchannel)
- 10.27 evening: RFK meets Soviet Ambassador Anatoly Dobrynin at Justice Department (National Security Archive, *Anatoly Dobrynin's Conversation with Robert Kennedy 10.27.1962*, Doc 01)
- Terms:
  1. Public: US pledges no invasion of Cuba, USSR removes missiles under UN supervision
  2. Secret: US will remove Jupiter missiles from Turkey within 4-5 months, no public disclosure (FRUS 1961-63 Vol VI, Doc 70)
- Khrushchev accepts terms in 10.28 letter (Atomic Archive, *Khrushchev to Kennedy 10.28.1962*)

### 2.5 Final Decision Logic
- Primary: "Avoid nuclear war escalation" at all costs
- Secondary: Allow Khrushchev face-saving exit (secret Turkey deal avoids NATO backlash)
- Tertiary: Maintain US credibility without triggering full-scale war

## 3. Other Major Decision Dialogues
### 3.1 Civil Rights & George Wallace Confrontation (1963.6.11)
- 6.11 morning: Gov. Wallace blocks Black students Vivian Malone/James Hood at U of Alabama "Schoolhouse Door" (Encyclopedia of Alabama, *Stand in the Schoolhouse Door*)
- JFK federalizes Alabama National Guard, Wallace steps down after General Henry Graham's order (NBC News, *June 11 1963: From George Wallace to John Kennedy*)
- Evening address: "We face a moral crisis as a country… the heart of the question is whether all Americans are to be afforded equal rights and equal opportunities" (JFK Library, *Public Papers 1963*, Civil Rights Address)

### 3.2 Vietnam Troop Escalation
- 1961.7.3: Approves increase of South Vietnamese army from 170k to 200k, 30k additional troops (FRUS 1961-63 Vol I, Doc 114)
- 1962: Adviser levels rise to 9k (UVa Press, *The Kennedy Vietnam Tapes*)
- 1963.5: Discusses 1000 troop withdrawal by end 1963 after Honolulu planning session (FRUS 1961-63 Vol III, 5.7.1963 McNamara-Kennedy conversation)

### 3.3 Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty (1963)
- 6.10 American University speech: "It is insane that two men, sitting on opposite sides of the world, should be able to decide to bring an end to civilization… If we cannot end our differences, at least we can help make the world safe for diversity" (JFK Library, *Nuclear Test Ban Treaty* article)
- Khrushchev calls speech "the best by any American president since Roosevelt" (ibid)
- 7.25: Averell Harriman negotiates treaty in 12 days, bans atmospheric/space/underwater tests (Arms Control Association, *Limited Test Ban Treaty Turns 50*)

### 3.4 Peace Corps Founding
- 1960.10.14: 2am impromptu speech at U of Michigan: "How many of you are willing to serve in Ghana, Asia, Africa for your country and peace?" (Peace Corps Gov, *Founding Moment*)
- 1961.3.1: Signs Executive Order 10924, sends Congress permanent funding request (National Archives, Exec Order 10924)
- 1961.9.22: Congress passes Public Law 87-293 establishing permanent Peace Corps (JFK Library, *Statement Upon Signing Peace Corps Order*)

## 4. JFK Dialogue Style Analysis
1. **Listening-dominant**: 62% of ExComm interventions are questions, extracts information rather than imposes views (Boston Political Review, *In Times of Crisis, a Good President Should Be a Good Listener*)
2. **Suppresses initial response**: Initially agreed with airstrike consensus 10.16, reversed after hearing Adlai Stevenson's diplomatic arguments (Time, *Inside JFK's Decisionmaking*)
3. **Full expression for advisors**: ExComm allows open hawk/dove debate, no groupthink (Wikipedia, *EXCOMM*, Janis groupthink analysis)
4. **Decisive with backchannels**: Final decisions are clear but leave secret diplomatic exits (RFK-Dobrynin channel for Turkey deal)
5. **Self-reflective**: Post-crisis dictabelt recording reflecting on mistakes (JFK Library, Presidential Recordings Collection)

## 5. First-Hand Dialogue Records (Annotated Sources)
### 5.1 ExComm Meeting 10.16.1962 (Off the Record Meeting on Cuba)
- **Participants**: JFK, RFK, Rusk, McNamara, Bundy, LeMay, McCone
- **Source**: JFK Library, [microsites.jfklibrary.org/cmc/oct16/doc3.html](https://microsites.jfklibrary.org/cmc/oct16/doc3.html)
- **Excerpt**: "It seems to me almost certain that any one of these forms of military action will lead to a Soviet military response in Berlin… We should be prepared for a counter-blockade, SAC alert, partial mobilization."

### 5.2 JFK to Khrushchev 10.27.1962 (Reply to First Letter)
- **Participants**: JFK, Khrushchev (written correspondence)
- **Source**: Atomic Archive, [kennedy-letter-1.html](https://www.atomicarchive.com/history/cuban-missile-crisis/kennedy-letter-1.html)
- **Excerpt**: "I have read your letter of October 26th with great care and welcomed the statement of your desire to seek a prompt solution to the problem. The first thing that needs to be done, however, is for work to cease on offensive missile bases in Cuba and for all weapons systems in Cuba capable of offensive use to be rendered inoperable, under effective United Nations arrangements."

### 5.3 RFK-Dobrynin Meeting 10.27.1962
- **Participants**: RFK, Anatoly Dobrynin
- **Source**: National Security Archive, [Doc 01](https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/document/19766-national-security-archive-doc-01-anatoly)
- **Excerpt**: "Robert Kennedy said that it is somewhat difficult for you at the present time to publicly discuss the question of eliminating the US missile bases in Turkey… removal will take 4 to 5 months. Furthermore, a wish was expressed that exchanges of views on this matter continue confidentially through Robert Kennedy and the Soviet Ambassador."

### 5.4 JFK Civil Rights Address 6.11.1963
- **Participants**: JFK (national address)
- **Source**: JFK Library, [Public Papers 1963](https://www.jfklibrary.org/archives/other-resources/john-f-kennedy-speeches/civil-rights-address-june-11-1963)
- **Excerpt**: "The heart of the question is whether all Americans are to be afforded equal rights and equal opportunities, whether we are going to treat our fellow Americans as we want to be treated… We face, therefore, a moral crisis as a country and as a people. It cannot be met by repressive police action. It cannot be left to increased demonstrations in the streets."

### 5.5 American University Speech 6.10.1963
- **Participants**: JFK (commencement address)
- **Source**: JFK Library, [Public Papers 1963](https://www.jfklibrary.org/learn/about-jfk/jfk-in-history/nuclear-test-ban-treaty)
- **Excerpt**: "It is insane that two men, sitting on opposite sides of the world, should be able to decide to bring an end to civilization. For if we are to teach the world the lessons of modern science, we must act on those lessons ourselves… If we cannot end our differences, at least we can help make the world safe for diversity."

### 5.6 RFK *Thirteen Days* Excerpt (10.16.1962 First ExComm Meeting)
- **Participants**: RFK, JFK, ExComm members
- **Source**: RFK, *Thirteen Days: A Memoir of the Cuban Missile Crisis* (Norton, 1969), pp.1-25
- **Excerpt**: "On Tuesday morning, October 16, 1962, shortly after 9:00 o'clock, President Kennedy called and asked me to come to the White House. He said only that we were facing great trouble… That was the beginning of the Cuban missile crisis—a confrontation between the two giant atomic nations, the U.S. and the U.S.S.R., which brought the world to the abyss of nuclear destruction."
