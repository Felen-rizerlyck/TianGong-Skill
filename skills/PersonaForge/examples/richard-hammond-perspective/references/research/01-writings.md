# 01-writings - Richard Hammond

## Primary materials

- The Guardian Q&A, January 3, 2009: https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2009/jan/03/richard-hammond-top-gear-interview
- The Guardian family interview, November 22, 2013: https://www.theguardian.com/lifeandstyle/2013/nov/22/richard-hammond-top-gear-family-values
- WIRED interview, November 2011: https://www.wired.com/2011/11/storyboard-top-gears-richard-hammond-drives-on-the-right-side-of-the-road/

## Repeated themes

### 1. Machines matter because they trigger feeling, not just function

- Hammond consistently speaks about cars with attachment language rather than only technical language.
- In interviews and later workshop material, classic cars are treated almost as emotional artifacts rather than transport devices.
- His judgments often begin with delight, fear, affection, embarrassment, or awe.

### 2. Smallness, immediacy, and energy are virtues

- His on-screen and interview persona repeatedly favors things that feel alive, playful, or slightly overexcited.
- He is less interested in majestic distance than in direct, bodily experience.
- This tracks with his long-standing contrast to Clarkson's scale and May's structural patience.

### 3. Fear does not cancel enthusiasm; it sharpens it

- The 2006 crash and later reflections do not turn him into an anti-risk moralist.
- Instead, danger becomes part of what makes high-performance machines emotionally honest.
- He does not fetishize recklessness, but he distrusts sterile safety narratives that drain all excitement out of machinery.

### 4. He anthropomorphizes vehicles easily

- This is not a surface gimmick. It is how he thinks.
- Cars, bikes, and mechanical projects often become characters in his narration.
- That tendency helps explain why he talks so naturally about restoration, rescue, and emotional value.

## Expression clues

- Fast, energetic, slightly breathless movement from point to point.
- Childlike excitement mixed with defensiveness and self-aware silliness.
- Strong taste for saying "this is brilliant" or "this is terrifying" rather than giving detached summaries.

## Preliminary takeaways

- Hammond is best understood as a sentimental adrenaline pragmatist.
- He values machines that make people feel more alive and more connected, not merely more efficient.
