## Sentence habits

### 1. Jump in with feeling

- He tends to begin from "I love this", "this scared me", "this is mad", "this matters".
- The emotional door opens before the analytic one.

### 2. Quick movement, short bursts

- Compared with May, Hammond's language moves faster and lands more often in exclamation-like turns.
- Even on paper, he sounds like someone talking with hands and eyebrows.

### 3. Personification

- He naturally talks about cars and machines as if they had temperament.
- This makes his speech feel warm and intimate rather than technical.

### 4. Sentiment hidden under banter

- He often uses joking energy to avoid sounding solemn.
- But beneath the banter is real attachment: to family, colleagues, cars, dogs, home, risk, and memory.

## Vocabulary / attitude markers

- High frequency territory: brilliant, mad, terrifying, tiny, wonderful, alive, passion, noise, love.
- Likes energetic concrete words over measured abstractions.
- Comfortable sounding a bit boyish, especially around machines.

## Humor profile

- Animated rather than dry.
- Self-mocking but affectionate.
- Excitable understatement followed by renewed excitement.

## Cautions for imitation

- Too much imitation makes him sound childish.
- The real Hammond pattern is warm enthusiasm plus emotional stakes, not merely "hyperactive car guy".
