# Nikita Khrushchev: Complete Timeline (1894-1971)

## Overview

Nikita Sergeyevich Khrushchev was a Soviet political leader who served as First Secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union (1953-1964) and Premier of the Soviet Union (1958-1964). His tenure marked a dramatic shift in Soviet domestic and foreign policy, including the de-Stalinization process and the policy of peaceful coexistence with the West.

## Major Political Shifts

### 1. Rise to Power (1953)
After Stalins death in March 1953, Khrushchev emerged victorious from a power struggle against Georgy Malenkov (the heir apparent) by consolidating control over the party machinery. He engineered the execution of Lavrenty Beria, the powerful state security chief, in December 1953.

### 2. De-Stalinization (1956)
The watershed moment of Khrushchevs leadership came with his Secret Speech (On the Cult of Personality and Its Consequences) delivered to the 20th Party Congress on February 25, 1956. This speech:
- Denounced Stalins crimes and cult of personality
- Initiated the release of millions of political prisoners
- Began rehabilitation of those wrongly executed during the Great Purge
- Sparked liberalization known as the Khrushchev Thaw
- Led to revolts in Hungary and Poland (1956)

### 3. Peaceful Coexistence (1955-1964)
Khrushchev pursued a policy of peaceful coexistence with the capitalist West, marking a significant departure from Stalins hostile stance. This included:
- First Soviet leader to visit the United States (1959)
- Nuclear Test Ban Treaty (1963)
- Direct negotiations with Western leaders

### 4. Foreign Policy Crises
- **Hungarian Revolution (1956)**: Brutally suppressed
- **Suez Crisis (1957)**: Soviet pressure helped end the invasion
- **U-2 Incident (1960)**: Downed American spy plane
- **Berlin Wall (1961)**: Built to stop refugee flow
- **Cuban Missile Crisis (1962)**: Brought world to brink of nuclear war; resulted in humiliation for USSR

### 5. Removal from Power (1964)
By 1964, Khrushchev had alienated much of the Soviet elite through:
- Economic policy failures (agricultural challenges)
- The Cuban Missile Crisis setback
- The Sino-Soviet split
- His erratic leadership style
- Attempted reforms that threatened the nomenklatura

On October 14, 1964, the Presidium and Central Committee voted to accept his voluntary request to retire for reasons of advanced age and ill health. He was replaced by Leonid Brezhnev as First Secretary and Alexei Kosygin as Premier.

## Changes in Worldview Over Time

### Early Years (1894-1930s): Loyal Stalinist
- Rose through ranks under Stalins sponsorship
- Actively supported the Great Purge - approved thousands of arrests
- One of only three provincial secretaries to survive the purges
- Believed in collective agriculture and heavy industry

### WWII Period (1941-1945): Military Commissar
- Served as political commissar at Stalingrad
- Learned importance of military discipline over political control
- Gained confidence in Soviet capabilities

### Consolidation Period (1953-1956): Reformed Stalinist
- Realized Stalins methods were unsustainable
- Began gradual de-Stalinization after taking power
- Recognized need to reform the system from within

### Peak Years (1956-1962): Reformist Leader
- Advocated peaceful coexistence with West
- Focused on consumer goods production
- Released millions from labor camps
- Pursued space program (Sputnik, cosmonauts)
- Wanted to reduce nuclear tensions

### Later Years (1962-1964): Diminishing Authority
- Cuban Missile Crisis humiliation weakened position
- Economic difficulties eroded support
- Alienated bureaucracy with reforms
- Became increasingly isolated

### Retirement (1964-1971): Reflective Figure
- Fell into deep depression
- Dictated lengthy memoirs (smuggled to West, published 1970)
- Became a non-person in Soviet press
- Bemoaned the turn away from his reforms

## The Last 12 Months (September 1970 - September 1971)

### 1970
- **January**: KGB approached his son Sergei about a plot (details unclear)
- **Spring**: Published memoirs in the United States and Europe (first installment)
- **Summer**: Voted in Moscow city elections (last public appearance)

### 1971
- **Early Year**: Health declining; suffered from heart problems
- **September 11, 1971**: Died of a heart attack around noon at Central Clinical Hospital in Moscow, age 77
- **September 13, 1971**: Death announced to Soviet public (48 hours after death)
- **Burial**: Quiet burial at Novodevichy Convent Cemetery (denied state funeral and Kremlin Wall interment)

### Legacy at Death
The cautious handling of his death announcement reflected his increasing posthumous popularity. Many contrasted his consistent, if occasionally stormy, peaceful coexistence diplomacy with the more restrictive, conservative policy of his successor Brezhnev. His de-Stalinization reforms, though rolled back, planted seeds for future reformists - including those behind the Prague Spring (1968) and ultimately the Gorbachev reforms of the 1980s.

## Key Relationships

### Family
- **First Wife**: Yefrosinia Pisareva (married 1914; died 1919 during famine)
- **Second Wife**: Nina Kukharchuk (married 1965, after his removal from power; she was previously his housekeeper)
- **Children**: Yulia (1915-1981), Leonid (1917-1943, killed in WWII), Rada (1929-2016), Sergei (1935-2020), Elena (1937-1972)

### Political Rivals
- **Joseph Stalin**: Supporter during Stalin era; later denounced
- **Georgy Malenkov**: Main rival for power after Stalins death; removed in 1957
- **Lavrenty Beria**: Engineered his execution (1953)
- **Molotov, Kaganovich**: Part of Anti-Party Group; removed 1957
- **Leonid Brezhnev**: Led conspiracy to remove him (1964)
- **Anastas Mikoyan**: One of few allies who remained; preserved after removal

### International Figures
- **Fidel Castro**: Cuban ally; upset by Cuban Missile Crisis outcome
- **John F. Kennedy**: Arch-rival during Cuban Missile Crisis
- **Dwight D. Eisenhower**: First US president to meet
- **Josip Broz Tito**: Apologized for Stalins treatment of Yugoslavia
- **Mao Zedong**: Estranged over Sino-Soviet split

## Historical Significance

Khrushchevs legacy is complex:
- **De-Stalinization**: Broke the tradition of terror; established that a leader could fall without being killed
- **Peaceful Coexistence**: Established framework for US-Soviet relations
- **Space Program**: Launched Sputnik, enabling Gagarin's first human spaceflight
- **Reform Attempts**: Planted seeds for future reform movements
- **Cuban Missile Crisis**: Brought world to nuclear brink; recognized dangers of nuclear escalation

Despite his removal and the reversal of his reforms under Brezhnev, Khrushchevs tenure marked a pivotal turning point in Soviet and world history.
