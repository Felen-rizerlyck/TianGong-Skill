# Nikita Khrushchev: Conversations and Interviews Research

## Overview

This document records Nikita Khrushchevs conversations with Western journalists diplomats and foreign leaders. These dialogues reveal his dual communication style: rigid and ideological in formal settings but casual and jocular in informal settings.

## II. Core Conversation Details

### 2.1 CBS Face the Nation TV Interview 1957

Background: In June 1957 Khrushchev became the first Soviet leader to accept a US TV camera crew in the Kremlin. CBS set up studio equipment and Khrushchev wore a light grey suit per their suggestion.

Key QA:

On troop cuts (asked about Soviet reduction of 1.8 million men):
Question: How many remain?
Khrushchev: I dont have accurate figures.

On Hungary:
Question: Wasnt Hungary against the will of the people?
Khrushchev: (fist clenching) Absolutely! Absolutely! How can it be otherwise?

On his famous prediction that Americas grandchildren would live under Socialism:
Khrushchev: (smiling pointing his finger) Your grandchildren in America will live under Socialism.

Khrushchevs reaction style:
- Almost never looking at notes improvising
- Using I dont have accurate figures as evasion
- The famous Your American communists can come to our kitchen joke

This was one of his most successful public appearances. Confident sometimes humorous facing sharp questions without回避 but not directly addressing key substance.

### 2.2 Eleanor Roosevelt Interview Yalta 1957

Background: In October 1957 former First Lady Eleanor Roosevelt interviewed Khrushchev at Yalta (then a Soviet vacation spot). This interview was notable because Khrushchev did not see the questions in advance and answered completely spontaneously.

Key answers:

On war and peace:
Roosevelt: We were not suspicious of the USSR at first. We fought together. I know my husband President Truman had real hope that we would be able to come to understandings.
Khrushchev: About the Yalta agreement we have different points of view as to who broke that agreement. We cannot agree

On Middle East arms sales:
Khrushchev: Who first started selling arms to those countrieswe or you? What about Pakistan?

Khrushchev: You dont only sell but supply free of charge! Who was first?

On Communist victory:
Khrushchev: I have never hidden myself from such questions. I told your CBS: Communism will win in the whole world. This is scientifically based on the writings of Marx and Engels

Khrushchevs improvisational style:
- Kicking the ball back to the questioner when answering
- Using counter-questions when refusing to yield
- Showing respect for President Roosevelt while insisting on disagreement

### 2.3 1959 Economic Club NY Speech Waldorf Astoria

Background: This was the core event of Khrushchevs 1959 US visit. After his speech he faced QandA from a panel of untested business elites.

Instant answer example:

On the camel joke:
Ambassador Lodge: I have been in this room many times and never seen it as full as it is tonight.
Khrushchev responded instantly: In my country there are many people who have never seen a camel. If you brought a camel to that part of the country they would all turn out. (Laughter and applause)

On QandA style:
- Almost every answer exceeding three minutes
- Often diverging to Communist superiority topic
- Showing anger at provocative questions (e.g. radio jamming)
- Using internal affairs to refuse questions about Soviet broadcasting jamming

Key QA:
Q: About radio jamming
Khrushchev: This question is entirely an internal matter. It is none of your business. If we persisted in an unreasonable attitude there would be no end of jamming.

Analysis: Khrushchev himself regarded this QandA as unsuccessful he felt the questions were too provocative.

### 2.4 1959 Private Conversation with Eisenhower

Background: During Khrushchevs September 1959 US visit he held secret meetings with President Eisenhower at Gettysburg and Camp David.

Conversation record from US State Department memcon:

On nuclear war:
Eisenhower: I am afraid of nuclear war. Everyone should be afraid.
Khrushchev: I am not for war but for peace.

On Germany:
Eisenhower: If two German states remain they will be an eternal hotbed of conflict.
Khrushchev: Quoted a Russian proverb: Policy is like a wagon tongue between two horses. (Untranslatable)

On Khrushchevs reaction:
- Calling Eisenhower old friend
- More casual in private
- Discussing specific issues rather than ideology

Analysis: Khrushchev was more moderate and pragmatic in private than in public.

### 2.5 1961 Vienna Summit with Kennedy

Background: In June 1961 Kennedy and Khrushchev met in Vienna. It was the only formal meeting between the two leaders.

Key conversation:

On Laos:
Kennedy: Why dont you let Laotian people live as they want to live?
Khrushchev: Well why dont you switch to our system? (half-joking)

On Germany and Berlin:
Khrushchev: We will sign a peace treaty this year.
Kennedy: That would be a cause for war.

On nuclear war danger:
Kennedy: Miscalculation is the greatest danger. Possibility of war between us through miscalculation.
Khrushchev: Yesbut Americans should recognize the danger too.

Analysis: Vienna produced no joint statement or substantive agreement. Khrushchev became more hawkish after the summit.

## III. Improvised Response Patterns

### 3.1 Typical Strategies for Tricky Questions

Strategy | Description | Typical Case
Counter-question | Not answering but turning question back | Who first sold arms?
Internal affairs | Using sovereignty to avoid | Jamming is internal matter
Pretending not to hear | Needing clarification | I lack accurate figures
Rising to ideology | Elevating specific to system debate | Your grandchildren will live under Socialism
Joking avoidance | Using humor to deflect | Camel joke Disneyland

### 3.2 Khrushchevs Jokes and Proverbs

Famous proverbs and metaphors:

When the shrimp learns to whistle we will abandon Communism. Kremlin dinner

You cannot force the buffalo to eat meat; the tiger cannot be made to eat grass. Bangalore India

We dont want people walking into our bedrooms. (explaining rejection of nuclear inspection) London trade unionists

Only the grave can correct a hunchback. (retorting about his stature)

Analysis: Most jokes were pre-prepared by two main speechwriters (Andrei Shevchenko and G.T. Shuisky). Khrushchev inserted these jokes in public to fill time or avoid questions.

## IV. Position Changes and Compromise Moments

### 4.1 1955 Geneva Eisenhower Open Skies Proposal

Background: At the 1955 Geneva Summit Eisenhower proposed Open Skies mutual aerial reconnaissance.

Khrushchevs instant response:
This is purely a propaganda move. Your proposal is impractical.

But the next day he privately met with Eisenhower:
I have a different opinion from my colleague (Bulganin)your proposal is actually impractical.

Analysis: Typical Khrushchev patternpublic hardline private flexible.

### 4.2 1957 Hungary From Non-Intervention to Intervention

In early October 1956 Hungarian revolution Khrushchev hesitated initially about intervention. But when he saw the uprising might spread he ordered Soviet troops in.

Khrushchev to US Ambassador Charles Bohlen: We will put in more troopsthe more the better

### 4.3 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis From Brink to Retreat

Key turning points:
- Secret missile deployment July 1962
- Discovered facing US response October 1962
- First letter: Commitment to withdraw (October 26)
- Second letter: Adding conditions (October 27)
- Final withdrawal accepting secret deal

Analysis: Khrushchev showed typical brinkmanshipblustering but retreating when facing real confrontation.

## V. Refused Questions and Avoidance Strategies

### 5.1 Refused Questions in Formal Settings

1. Specific Soviet troop numbers: Using I dont have accurate figures
2. Technical details of broadcast jamming: Internal affaircompletely none of your business
3. Names of Eastern European countries in Soviet maps: Refusing to discuss enslaved nations
4. Specific dissident cases: Using USSR has no dissidents

### 5.2 Avoidance Strategy Analysis

Khrushchevs most common avoidance strategies:

A. Turning the Tables:
- Not answering but asking a more basic related question
- When asked about weapons counter-asking who started first

B. Rising to Philosophy:
- Turning specific questions into system superiority debates
- You Americans misunderstand Communism

C. Citing Soviet Wisdom:
- Using proverbs or sayings to generalize
- Policy is like wagon tongue between two horses

D. Pretending Humor:
- Using jokes to change topic
- Using laughter to fill silence when necessary

## VI. Khrushchev Communication Style DNA

### 6.1 Formal vs Informal Dual Personality

Setting | Performance | Example
Formal press conference | Rigid ideological | Reading prepared speech
Informal reception | Casual joking | Camel story
TV interview | Semi-improvised sometimes humorous | CBS 1957
Private dinner | Pragmatic private commitments | Camp David Eisenhower
面对学生 | Confident sometimes provocative | 1960 US tour

### 6.2 Core Patterns

1. Dual-Track Communication:
- Internal: Flexible pragmatism
- External: Rigid ideology

2. Brinkmanship Enthusiast:
- Blustering in public
- Avoiding direct confrontation in private

3. Improvised vs Prepared:
- Real improvisation in small informal settings
- Heavy pre-preparation in large formal settings

4. Language Characteristics:
- Preferring peasant proverbs
- Repeating key words (absolutely)
- Using gestures (fist clenching pointing finger)
