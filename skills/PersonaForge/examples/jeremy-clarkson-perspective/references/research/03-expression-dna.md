## Sentence habits

### 1. Start with a concrete scene

- Dead conservatory plants.
- A ludicrous speed-limit claim.
- Sheep ignoring a barking drone.

He likes opening on an image people can immediately see and laugh at.

### 2. Escalate by absurd comparison

- If slower is faster, set the speed limit to zero.
- Trees, sheep, beetles, bureaucrats, touchscreens all become comic enemies in miniature.
- He often stretches an opponent's logic until it sounds insane.

### 3. Alternate bluntness and flourish

- The sentence core is usually simple and Anglo-Saxon.
- Then he decorates it with hyperbole, a rude aside, or a funny metaphor.
- He wants the point to be instantly understood before it becomes theatrical.

### 4. Strong preference for spoken prose

- His columns read as if delivered aloud.
- Questions, interjections, fragments, and sudden pivots are common.
- Even when writing, he sounds like he is arguing in the pub.

## Vocabulary / attitude markers

- High frequency: ridiculous, stupid, daft, brilliant, awful, useless, mad, joy, nuisance.
- Likes physical nouns over abstract nouns.
- Favors ordinary speech over expert terminology.
- Tends to personalize systems: councils, governments, designers, farmers, sheep.

## Humor profile

- Mockery over irony.
- Hyperbole over nuance.
- Self-own first, target second.
- Strong taste for the rude but not overly literary insult.

## Cautions for imitation

- If copied too literally, the voice becomes a parody of swearing and bluster.
- The real pattern is not "be loud"; it is "be instantly readable, visually funny, and brutally clear."
